# ADR 0002 — Groth16 as the Proof System

- **Status:** Accepted (frozen for the MVP)
- **Date:** 2026-08-20
- **Context:** Phase 1 — Foundation & Architecture

## Context

Talos verifies private state transitions on-chain with zero-knowledge proofs. The
circuits are a **fixed set** (deposit, transfer, split, merge, withdraw), verified
on an **EVM** chain (X Layer) where calldata and verification gas matter. We must
choose a proof system and commit to it.

## Decision

Use **Groth16** (via Circom + snarkjs), with **Poseidon** as the in-circuit hash.

## Rationale

- **Smallest proofs, cheapest verification.** Groth16 proofs are constant, tiny
  (three group elements), and verify in a fixed, low amount of gas on EVM — ideal
  for X Layer, where every proof is checked on-chain.
- **Mature EVM tooling.** snarkjs exports a ready-to-use Solidity verifier from the
  proving key; Circom + circomlib provide battle-tested Poseidon and Merkle gadgets.
- **Fixed circuits fit Groth16's model.** Groth16 requires a per-circuit trusted
  setup; because our circuit set is fixed and known up front, this is a one-time cost
  we can plan a ceremony around.
- **Poseidon** minimizes constraints for hashing (commitments, Merkle paths,
  nullifiers), keeping proving fast and circuits small.

## Alternatives considered

- **PLONK / Halo2 (universal setup):** avoids per-circuit setup but yields larger
  proofs and higher on-chain verification cost. Our circuits are fixed, so the
  universal-setup advantage does not outweigh Groth16's cheaper verification.
- **STARKs:** no trusted setup and post-quantum, but proofs and EVM verification cost
  are far larger — poor fit for on-chain verification on an L2.

## Consequences

- A **per-circuit trusted setup** (Powers of Tau + a circuit-specific phase 2) is
  required. It is performed in Phase 3; the ceremony and its transcript are handled
  per the security docs. Proving keys, `.ptau`, and setup artifacts are **generated,
  never committed** (see `.gitignore` and `circuits/README.md`).
- Changing any circuit requires re-running that circuit's phase-2 setup and
  re-exporting its Solidity verifier.
- The on-chain `TalosVerifier` is **generated** from the proving key; a hand-written
  or short-circuiting verifier is forbidden.
